How search profiles work

Each profile is a named PubMed query that runs on your fetch schedule. Papers returned by any of your profiles are added to your library, deduplicated by PubMed ID. You can have as many profiles as you like, and enable or disable them individually without deleting them.

Writing a good query

  • Use [MeSH] to match controlled vocabulary terms: "Artificial Intelligence"[MeSH]
  • Combine terms with AND, OR, and NOT: ("machine learning"[MeSH] OR "deep learning") AND "clinical microbiology"
  • Search specific fields with tags: [tiab] for title and abstract, [au] for author, [jour] for journal
  • Phrase search with quotes: "antimicrobial resistance"

Use PubMed Advanced Search to build and test queries interactively before pasting them here.

Relevance tracking

Each paper in your feed that was fetched by a named search profile shows thumbs-up () and thumbs-down () buttons. Use them to tell the system whether the paper was a relevant result. You can also rate papers from the paper detail page. Clicking a button a second time clears the rating.

Because a paper can be matched by more than one search profile, a single rating counts as feedback for every profile that fetched it. When a profile's relevant percentage falls below your alert threshold and enough papers have been rated, a warning appears at the top of your feed. Per-profile stats are shown within each profile below.

Changes take effect on the next fetch run.

How topic classification works

Papers are classified by matching their MeSH headings and author keywords against the terms you assign to each topic. A paper can belong to more than one topic. Papers with no matching terms show as Unclassified. Changes take effect immediately without re-fetching.

MeSH headings are controlled vocabulary assigned by NLM indexers — use the NLM MeSH Browser for exact headings. Author keywords are free-text from authors and match exactly as written. Recent papers may lack MeSH terms; author keywords fill this gap until indexing is complete.
